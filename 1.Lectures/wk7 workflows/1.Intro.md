# Lecture: Introduction to Provenance

## ✅ Summary

This lecture sets the stage for understanding **data provenance**—the origin and transformation history of data—as part of the broader **data cleaning and analysis lifecycle**. It highlights how data cleaning is just one step in a larger pipeline, where automation, reproducibility, and workflow tracking become crucial.

Key ideas:
- Data cleaning involves multiple steps: from syntax corrections (regex, OpenRefine) to schema-level checks (Datalog).
- Researchers often **automate** data processing using tools like **Python**, **R**, or scientific workflow systems.
- **Provenance** is the record of data’s **origin, transformations, and the steps applied**.
- Provenance helps with:
  - Reproducibility
  - Debugging
  - Understanding how results were derived

---

## 📚 Details

### 🔸 Context of Provenance

- Data cleaning and analysis involve **multiple stages**:
  - Initial corrections (regex, OpenRefine)
  - Schema-level integrity checks (Datalog)
  - Logical and semantic validation

- **Automation** is a key requirement:
  - Cleaning tasks are often scripted.
  - Full analyses involve multi-step pipelines (e.g., loading, transforming, aggregating).

---

### 🔸 Why Provenance Matters

- Researchers need to know:
  - **Where data came from**
  - **What operations were applied**
  - **Who or what performed these steps**
- Provenance is critical for:
  - **Reproducibility**: Can someone else replicate your analysis?
  - **Debugging**: Can you trace where an error was introduced?
  - **Transparency**: Can you explain your results to stakeholders?

---

### 🔸 Provenance in Practice

- Typically managed through:
  - **Scientific workflow systems** (e.g., Kepler, Taverna, Galaxy)
  - **Script-based automation** (e.g., Python, R pipelines)
  - Tools like **YesWorkflow** to annotate and visualize scripts

---

> 🧠 Provenance isn’t just a "nice-to-have"—it’s a **core component** of trustworthy, reproducible data science.
