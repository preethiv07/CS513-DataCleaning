# Lecture: Introduction to Provenance

## ✅ Summary

This lecture introduces the concept of **provenance**—the origin and history of data and artifacts—as it applies to both natural and computational sciences. Provenance plays a central role in **ensuring transparency and reproducibility** in data-driven research. The lecture distinguishes **prospective provenance** (the intended workflow) from **retrospective provenance** (what actually happened). It sets the stage for deeper exploration of provenance tools and examples like **workflow systems** and the **DataONE project**.

---

## 📚 Details

### 🔸 What is Provenance?

- **Provenance** refers to the **origin and transformation history** of data or artifacts.
- In **natural sciences**, provenance shows up in areas such as:
  - **Phylogeny** in biology (evolutionary tree of life)
  - **Cosmology** in astronomy (origins of the universe)
  - **Genealogy** in human history (family trees)
  - **Etymology** in linguistics (origins of words)

---

### 🔸 Provenance in Data Science

- Focus is on **computational provenance**:
  - Tracing how raw data was processed
  - Understanding intermediate steps
  - Assessing **data quality** by inspecting the process, not just the result

- Enables:
  - **Transparency**: making the full data process visible
  - **Reproducibility**: allowing others to repeat the process and verify results

---

### 🔸 Prospective vs. Retrospective Provenance

| Type                  | Description |
|-----------------------|-------------|
| **Prospective**       | The intended "recipe" or **workflow** that shows how data should be processed |
| **Retrospective**     | The actual log or trace of what happened during data processing |

In this week's videos, focus will be on **prospective provenance** via **workflow systems**.
